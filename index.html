Research

PhD research project

Multilingual information extraction in low-resource settings

Much of the information about African territories exists only as text and speech: administrative reports, local press, project reports and residents' accounts in national languages. These sources mention villages, tracks, markets and rivers that are missing from GeoNames and OpenStreetMap, or wrongly placed there.

I propose methods to recognize, resolve and ground spatial entities in French texts produced in Togo and in speech transcripts in Ewe and Kabiye, and to estimate the location of places absent from gazetteers from spatial relations in text, such as "5 km north of Kpalimé".

Planned outputs: an annotated corpus and benchmark, evaluated models for low-resource spatial entity recognition and resolution, and a geographic knowledge graph validated against field data, including Togo's national rural roads database.

Research questions

  • Can large language models, guided by a few examples and a spatial annotation schema, recognize local place names better than NER models trained on European corpora?
  • Can spatial relations expressed in text locate places absent from any gazetteer with usable accuracy?
  • Does stratified evaluation (urban vs. rural, origin language of the place name) reveal performance gaps hidden by global metrics?

Projects

Master's thesis · 2023 – 2024

Automating candidate preselection in online recruitment

An end-to-end NLP pipeline that ranks résumés against job descriptions: PDF text extraction (PyMuPDF), preprocessing, Word2Vec embeddings and cosine-similarity ranking, deployed as a Flask web application for non-technical users.

Evaluated against recruiter ground truth on three job postings: 100% precision and an NDCG of 0.99 on the top 3 candidates. Estimated screening time for 100 résumés drops from about 5 hours to about 20 seconds. The thesis also analyzes algorithmic bias, static versus contextual embeddings and data-privacy ethics.

Advisor: Dr. Amana Maniyassouwe, Collège de Paris Supérieur.
